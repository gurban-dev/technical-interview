# How to Solve Coding Challenges: A Beginner's Guide

## 1. Follow These Five Steps

Whenever you encounter a LeetCode or HackerRank problem, follow this process:

1. **Understand the problem.** Identify the input, expected output, constraints, and special cases. Explain the problem in your own words.
2. **Work through an example.** Solve a small example manually and determine the steps needed to reach the answer.
3. **Identify the pattern.** Ask yourself which algorithm or data structure might help.
4. **Write a simple solution first.** Start with a straightforward approach, even if it is inefficient. Once it works, look for ways to improve it.
5. **Test and optimize.** Check edge cases, fix mistakes, and analyze time and space complexity.

## 2. Learn the Most Common Patterns

Many coding challenges use a relatively small collection of recurring techniques.

- **Arrays and Strings:** Process sequences, reverse strings, find maximum values, and check palindromes.
- **Hash Maps and Sets:** Count occurrences, detect duplicates, and find matching values.
- **Two Pointers:** Examine two positions in a sequence to find pairs or check palindromes.
- **Sliding Window:** Analyze a continuous section of an array or string.
- **Binary Search:** Search efficiently in sorted data or suitable search spaces.
- **Stacks and Queues:** Process elements in a specific order, such as validating parentheses or traversing trees.
- **Trees and Graphs:** Explore connected nodes using Depth-First Search (DFS) or Breadth-First Search (BFS).
- **Backtracking:** Explore possible combinations, permutations, or solutions.
- **Greedy Algorithms:** Make locally optimal choices when they lead to a globally optimal solution.
- **Dynamic Programming:** Store and reuse solutions to smaller, overlapping subproblems.

Learn these patterns one at a time. Focus on understanding when and why each pattern works.

## 3. How to Practise

For each problem:

1. Attempt it independently before looking at the solution.
2. If you get stuck, study the explanation and understand the reasoning.
3. Close the solution and implement it yourself.
4. Revisit the problem a few days later and solve it again from memory.

Keep a journal recording the problem, its pattern, your approach, its time complexity, and what you learned.

Start with easy problems involving arrays, strings, dictionaries, and sets. Gradually progress to more advanced patterns.

## 4. Remember These Principles

- Do not memorize code. Learn the underlying algorithm.
- Do not optimize before you understand the problem.
- Always test edge cases.
- Learn to explain your reasoning aloud, as you would in an interview.
- Practice consistently and review your mistakes.

**The ultimate goal is not to memorize solutions. It is to recognize familiar patterns and use them to solve unfamiliar problems independently.**
